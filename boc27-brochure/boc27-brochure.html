About the Conference

Where competition law meets the realities of business

Business of Competition returns to Prague in April 2027 for its second edition, bringing together competition lawyers, economists, regulators, in-house counsel, litigation funders and other professionals working across European competition law.

The conference is built around practical discussion — current cases, enforcement priorities, litigation strategy, economics and the business consequences of competition law developments.

Following the 2026 edition, the programme will expand the involvement of corporate counsel and continue to bring together different sides of the market for two days of focused discussions and meaningful networking.

03  Business of Competition 2027
Programme

Day One · Morning

Thursday 8 April 2027
08.30 – 09.00Registration and welcome coffee
09.00 – 09.10

Opening remarks

A short welcome and introduction to the main questions guiding the conference.

09.10 – 09.30Opening keynote

The direction of European competition policy

A senior European Commission representative will set out the priorities shaping European competition policy and enforcement in 2027, and what companies and practitioners should prepare for next.

09.30 – 10.20Opening panel

Competition enforcement in a changing Europe

Building on the keynote, the discussion will look at how enforcement priorities are changing in practice and what this means for companies, advisers and markets across Europe.

10.20 – 10.50Networking break
10.50 – 11.40Expert discussion

Article 102 under the new Guidelines

A practical look at how the new Guidelines may change the assessment of exclusionary conduct, evidence and economic effects, drawing on recent EU and national cases.

11.40 – 12.30Case discussion

The Google cases in practice

Using Google Shopping, AdTech, Android, the DMA and Heureka, the speakers will explore the relationship between public enforcement, regulation and private damages claims in digital markets.

12.30 – 13.40Lunch
13.40 – 14.35Case discussion

Cartels beyond price fixing

The session will look beyond traditional price fixing to no-poach agreements, information exchange, minority shareholdings, algorithmic coordination and other risks companies may not immediately recognise.

05  Business of Competition 2027
Programme

Day Two

Friday 9 April 2027
09.00 – 09.20Welcome coffee
09.20 – 10.15Case discussion

Competition law and the changing rules of sport

Recent cases involving FIFA, CD Tondela, RRC Sports, Rogon, FIGC and CONI provide the basis for a practical discussion about player mobility, labour markets and the powers of sporting bodies.

10.15 – 11.10Case clinic

When economics changes the case

Economists and lawyers will work through a real or hypothetical competition case to show how economic analysis affects the theory of harm, evidence, causation, damages, remedies and settlement.

11.10 – 11.40Networking break
11.40 – 12.40Judicial roundtable

What judges need from competition cases

Judges from different European jurisdictions will compare their approaches to economic evidence, expert disagreement, disclosure, causation and damages, and discuss how complex cases could be presented more effectively.

12.40 – 13.50Lunch
13.50 – 14.45Practical roundtable

Investment screening across Europe

Using Czechia, Poland and Slovakia as practical examples, the session will examine how investors can manage different national requirements, parallel filings, sensitive sectors and transaction risk across Europe.

14.45 – 15.40In-house roundtable

Competition litigation risks and how companies can prepare

Company representatives and advisers will discuss how businesses can identify potential exposure, preserve evidence, coordinate internal teams and prepare for follow-on and collective claims across jurisdictions.

15.40 – 16.05Networking break
16.05 – 16.45Closing discussion

Five predictions for competition law

Five contributors will each identify one development that companies, advisers and authorities should prepare for over the coming year, followed by a live audience vote.

16.45 – 16.55

Closing remarks

A short conclusion bringing together the main ideas from both days and closing the conference.

Programme subject to change. Speakers to be announced.
